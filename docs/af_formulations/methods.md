# Methods: season-classified practice effects from ERA maize trials

*Companion to the interactive page (`docs/af_formulations/index.html`, Part 2 and Section 3). Written to match the code in `code/explore/af_formulations/` on `CC4A-SSA/cc4adapt` `main`. Every step names its script; the code-trace table at the end lists inputs, outputs and pins. Exploratory analysis; counts are exact, estimates are illustrative. Revised 31 October 2026 after an independent traceability review.*

## 1. Study design

We asked whether the yield effect of two agronomic practices, cover crops and reduced tillage, differs between climate-stress seasons and normal seasons at African maize trial sites, and whether that effect scales with yield level (a proportional response) or is constant in absolute terms (an additive response). The answers determine how a practice's benefit should be partitioned between a production component and an adaptation component when the partition follows the CGIAR climate-adaptation indicator (MELIAF working document v3.0, July 2026). We also compared the frequency of stress seasons derived from observed climate at the trial sites with the hazard frequencies published by the Africa Agriculture Adaptation Atlas for the districts containing those sites, and traced the difference to its sources using climate-model output.

Four parts: (i) assembly of trial comparisons with site coordinates and seasons; (ii) classification of each trial season as stressed or not, by hazard and severity, from daily climate records run through a crop water balance; (iii) estimation of practice effects separately in stressed and normal seasons, and a test of proportional against additive scaling; (iv) comparison of site-level stress frequencies with Atlas district frequencies, on observed and on climate-model climate. The Atlas's own per-season classification is not public and was not used; every stress season here was computed Atlas-style from weather records or climate-model output.

## 2. Trial data (`03b_era_site_years.R`)

Trial comparisons were drawn from the Evidence for Resilient Agriculture database (ERA; Rosenstock et al. 2024), file `ERA_Derived.rda` as held in this repository (md5 and fetch record in `data/staging/af_formulations/era_pin.json`; versions 2018 and 2020). ERA compiles paired treatment–control observations from published African field trials with site coordinates, measurement year and, where reported, planting and harvest dates.

We selected maize crop-yield comparisons whose practice label was exactly "Green Manure" (taken as the cover-crop practice; it pools ERA's nitrogen-fixing intercrops, 221 rows, nitrogen-fixing rotational or relay legumes, 132, and non-fixing legumes, 3) or exactly "Reduced Tillage" (no-till, 494 rows, and minimum tillage, 395). Exact matching on the practice label means the practice is the sole difference between the two arms; comparisons in which the practice is bundled with mulch, residue retention, rotation or other practices on one side only were excluded. Management common to both arms (fertiliser, improved varieties) is retained and varies between studies. Rows flagged by ERA as invalid comparisons, and rows with a missing or non-positive treatment or control mean, were dropped. Yields reported in kg/ha (204 rows) were converted to Mg/ha so that all rows share one unit. Thirty-two comparisons from irrigated trials were flagged and excluded from the season analysis (Section 6), since water stress at an irrigated plot is not observable in the climate record.

The resulting set comprises 1,245 comparisons from 106 studies at 111 sites, measured between 1971 and 2016 (29 comparisons lack a year). Per practice: reduced tillage 889 comparisons, 79 studies, 78 sites; cover crops 356, 31 and 40. There are 327 unique site-years, 287 of them from 1981 onwards, the period with daily rainfall records. Each comparison carries ERA's site key, a location-precision radius (median 0.9 km; 73 % of sites within 1 km, 81 % within 5 km, 14 sites beyond 50 km), the measurement year, and at bimodal sites ERA's season index (1 or 2) identifying which rainy season the trial occupied; the index is missing for 56 % of comparisons, which are treated as season 1. Planting and harvest dates are both present for 27 % of site-years.

Practice effects are expressed as the log response ratio, ln(Y_T / Y_C), of treatment to control yield (Hedges et al. 1999). The database's precomputed effect columns were not used: in this file version `yi` holds the plain ratio and `pc` is offset by 100 points (reported as `ERAgriculture/eragri#9`).

## 3. Growing-season windows (`03c_era_site_windows.R`)

Stress indices are defined within a growing season, so each site × season slot (128 combinations) required a window. Windows were assigned in order of preference:

1. **Reported dates** (33 site-slots). Where ERA recorded both planting and harvest dates, the modal planting month-day and modal harvest month-day across the site-slot's dated site-years define the window. These windows run 90–214 days.
2. **Rainfall climatology** (95 site-slots). From the WorldClim 2.1 monthly precipitation climatology (10 arc-minute; Fick and Hijmans 2017) at the site, a month is wet if its mean precipitation is ≥ 60 mm; a run of ≥ 2 consecutive wet months, scanning the circular year from the driest month, is a rainy season; the window runs from the first day of the run's first month to the last day of the month after the run, capped at five months. A site wet in every month receives its wettest five consecutive months. Where two runs occur, ERA season 1 is matched to the earlier and season 2 to the later. Thirteen season-2 site-slots lie at sites with a single climatological run; they were scored on that run and flagged (`window_status = slot_missing`).
3. **Crop calendar.** As a fallback (never triggered), and as the deliberate alternative in a second set of runs, the GGCMI Phase 3 rainfed-maize calendar (v1.01; Jägermeyr et al. 2021) at the nearest grid point, planting day to maturity day. This is the single-season calendar the Atlas applies to every crop and location. In the calendar runs all trials are scored on that one window, so second-season trials at bimodal sites (193 comparisons from 1981) have no matching season and drop out of the calendar-window results.

Seasons are attributed to the year in which the window starts, matching ERA's measurement-year convention. For windows crossing 31 December, step 07 fetches climate data through 30 June of the following year.

## 4. Climate records and hazard indices (`07_era_seasons.py`)

Daily precipitation was taken from CHIRPS v3 (0.05°; Funk et al. 2026) and daily maximum and minimum temperature from AgERA5 (0.1°; Copernicus Climate Change Service 2020), both read through Google Earth Engine for the grid cell containing each site, using the CGIAR climate-toolkit (CGIAR Climate Data Hub; commit 453c12a, July 2026; function `evaluate_hazards()`).

For each site × slot the toolkit ran a daily root-zone soil-water balance over every year's window with a 60-day spin-up: reference evapotranspiration by Hargreaves–Samani (1985) from Tmax and Tmin; maize crop coefficients 0.3, 1.2 and 0.6 for initial, mid and late season and a depletion fraction of 0.55 (the toolkit's FAO-56 starter set; Allen et al. 1998); rooting depth as the lesser of the site's HWSD root depth and 1.5 m; plant-available water capacity from SoilGrids field capacity and wilting point over that depth, with a coarse-fragment correction, limited to 25–400 mm; and a saturation store from SoilGrids porosity minus field capacity, limited to 20–300 mm. The values the toolkit used for each site are written to every output row (`soilcp_mm`, `soilsat_mm`, `root_depth_m`, `kc_*`, `depletion_p`). From the balance it counted, within the window: water-stress days (NDWS), days on which the ratio of actual to potential crop evapotranspiration fell below 0.5; waterlogging days (NDWL0), days with soil water above field capacity; and heat days (NTx35), days with maximum temperature ≥ 35 °C. These are the three generic indices behind the Atlas's dry, wet and heat classes.

## 5. Severity classification (`07_era_seasons.py`, `BANDS`)

The Atlas classes a season by the mean monthly count of index days over the season's months against fixed thresholds: NDWS > 15, 20 and 25 days per month for moderate, severe and extreme; NDWL0 > 2, 5 and 8; NTx35 > 7, 14 and 21. To reproduce those classes from season totals, each count was scaled to a monthly mean by multiplying by 365.25/12 and dividing by the site-slot's mean window length in days, then compared with the same thresholds. A season is a stress season for a hazard when its band is at or above the chosen threshold (moderate in the main analysis; severe as a sensitivity). A season may be a stress season for more than one hazard; hazards are analysed separately.

Two runs were made: on the site windows of Section 3 (279 site-seasons at 106 sites; the 5 other sites have no season from 1981) and on the GGCMI windows (253 site-seasons), identical in all other respects except the loss of second-season trials noted above.

## 6. Practice effects by season type (`08_era_split.R`)

Rainfed comparisons were joined to their site-season classification by site key, season slot and year. For each practice and hazard, log response ratios were averaged within study separately over stressed and normal seasons, then averaged across studies with equal weight and back-transformed to a percentage change; the median across studies is also written. The difference between the stress-season and normal-season estimates is the climate-specific effect in the sense of the CGIAR indicator (g_stress − g_norm). Study and site-season counts accompany every estimate. No precision weighting or publication-bias adjustment was applied. The yield gain used in Part 1 of the page ("ERA, pooled") is a different estimator: the median of study-mean log ratios over all seasons (`03_params.R`).

## 7. Proportional versus additive scaling (`08_era_split.R`)

Under a proportional-loss model the practice protects a share of yield, so in stress seasons the log response ratio should not depend on the control yield; under an additive model it protects a fixed quantity, so the yield difference in Mg/ha should not. Using stress-season comparisons at the moderate threshold only, we fitted, for each practice and hazard, (a) ln(Y_T / Y_C) on ln(Y_C) and (b) (Y_T − Y_C) on Y_C by ordinary least squares, with standard errors clustered by study (CR1 sandwich with the G/(G−1)·(n−1)/(n−k) correction). The model whose slope is closer to zero is the better description; when neither slope reaches |t| ≥ 2 the case is reported as ambiguous. Minimum requirements: 10 comparisons from 3 studies. The two regressions are not symmetric tests and power is low; results are indicative. An earlier version of this test mixed kg/ha and Mg/ha rows; the unit harmonisation in Section 2 corrects it.

## 8. Comparison with Atlas hazard frequencies (`09_obs_vs_atlas.R`, using `01`, `04`, `05`)

Each trial site was assigned to its GAUL 2024 second-level administrative unit (district) by point-in-polygon. For sites in maize-producing districts of Kenya, Ethiopia and Nigeria (districts with MapSPAM 2020 maize production, a finite yield and a usable Atlas row), the Atlas's hazard frequency for the district was recovered from the published hazard × exposure tables (`africa-hazard-exposure-nexgddp`, production tier, scenario `historic`, period `jagermeyr`, 18-model NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 ensemble mean, hazard set `NDWS+NTx35+NDWL0`; S3 object versions pinned in `01_fetch_inputs.R`) as the share of maize production in the four dry-containing classes over total production, i.e. the production-weighted mean frequency of seasons at or above the band within the district (`05_compute.R`). Ratios were clipped to [0, 1] and tiers forced monotone; a district's own row was used when complete and backed by at least 100 t of maize, otherwise its province's row (recorded as `p_source`; about a third of district–hazard pairs). This was compared with the share of classified trial seasons at or above the same band at the sites in that district, on both window definitions; country summaries weight sites by their number of seasons.

## 9. Attribution of the Atlas–observed difference (`10_gcm_vs_obs.py`, `11_gcm_summary.R`)

For the 15 Kenyan sites inside maize districts (one with a 25 km location radius), the same GGCMI window and toolkit water balance were run over the 19 seasons 1995–2013 (the Atlas baseline is 1995–2014; the 2014 window crosses into 2015, outside the NEX-GDDP historical period) with three climate inputs: the CHIRPS v3 + AgERA5 records for every season, not only trial seasons; and the historical runs of two Atlas ensemble members, ACCESS-CM2 and GFDL-ESM4, from NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 v1.1 (Thrasher et al. 2022) as served by Earth Engine. A fourth series, the trial seasons only, is taken from the GGCMI-window classification of Section 5. The difference between the observed and climate-model frequencies isolates the effect of the climate input; the difference between the climate-model frequencies and the Atlas value bounds the effect of the Atlas's own water balance, thresholds and spatial aggregation.

## 10. Software and reproducibility

Analyses were run in R 4.6.0 (data.table, arrow, duckdb, terra, sf, ggplot2) and Python 3.12 (pandas, climate-toolkit with earthengine-api and xee). All scripts, the pins (S3 object versions for Atlas products, md5 for the AEZ raster and the ERA file, toolkit commit) and the checks a re-run must pass are in `code/explore/af_formulations/` (MIT); outputs are CC BY 4.0. Automated checks cover the formulation algebra (`tests/testthat/test_formulations.R`) and the pilot's data invariants (`test_pilot_invariants.R`: one yield unit, one window per site-slot, bands consistent with thresholds, parameters recorded, matched comparisons rainfed). Not pinned: CHIRPS v3, AgERA5 and NEX-GDDP as served by Earth Engine on the run dates (October 2026), and the toolkit's local fetch cache, which is outside the repository. Of 237 site-slot fetches, 2 failed on transient network errors and were re-run; the Dixon farming-system rasters used in Part 1 are a local copy.

## 11. Limitations

The season window is the dominant known source of error: rescoring the same seasons on the GGCMI calendar instead of site windows raised the share of stress seasons by factors of 1.1 to 4.7 by country (Malawi ×4.7 on a 195-day calendar; Nigeria ×4 on a 95-day one) and reversed the sign of the stress-minus-normal difference for cover crops, though the calendar runs also lose second-season trials. Seventy-three per cent of site-years lack both planting and harvest dates. Site coordinates carry radii from 12 m to 500 km against 5–10 km climate cells; gridded rainfall is uncertain in the East African highlands. Water-balance parameters are the toolkit's defaults and uncalibrated. Scaling season totals by mean window length approximates the Atlas's calendar-month averaging. Stress-season estimates for cover crops rest on 10 studies at the moderate threshold and 2 at the severe one; heat stress was too rare to estimate on site windows. Controls span unfertilised to fertilised management; trials may under-sample failed seasons (not evident in the Kenyan check). Two of 18 ensemble members were run. The control's own stress-season loss cannot be estimated from ERA, which has no same-site normal-year control, so the loss share used in Part 1 remains a placeholder.
